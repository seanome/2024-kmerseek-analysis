kmerseek Dark Set 0.4

The dark set of a proteome is every protein that phmmer, jackhmmer and MMseqs2 all fail to match to anything in a reference database that leaves out the protein's own clade. This page asks how many such proteins each kmerseek setting puts a region on, and whether that setting also reaches the proteins those tools did match, so that a reach on dark proteins means something.

4 · Before reading these numbers as rescues

Caveats

  • The low-complexity mask was off for every setting on this page, so a region can rest on a stretch made of few kinds of residue (for example lysine-rich or serine-rich), which shares k-mers with many unrelated proteins.
  • Reached dark proteins lean towards low-complexity sequence in human and towards membrane helices in worm (notebook 279, PR 120): in those species a reached dark protein is more often annotated by UniProt as compositionally biased (human) or as having transmembrane helices (worm) than the dark set as a whole.
  • Reach is not correctness. A region on a dark protein says kmerseek found shared k-mers with some target; it does not say the target is a homolog.
  • BHF, the Botryllus histocompatibility factor, is not rescued. Its best kmerseek region (hp_lehninger2, k = 17) has E-value 2.97, a chance-level score, while phmmer finds SIV Vpx (P27984) at E-value 0.16. So BHF is placed, not dark, at a cutoff of 1 or 10.
  • Refused settings are missing, not zero. settings per species (both scaled values) have no fitted E-value statistics in their index and were not searched (grey × in 2c).
  • Botryllus groups come from protein names. The genome annotation named some proteins after a human gene (FUN000002_NLRC5); those take that human gene's HGNC group. Unnamed proteins have no group.
