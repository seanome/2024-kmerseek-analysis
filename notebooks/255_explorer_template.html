Ranking metrics against human Pfam domains

kmerseek scores every matched region in several ways. This page asks which score puts correct Pfam domain matches at the top of the list, and whether any does better than the length of the matched region alone. Match and correct match

A match is one pair of matched spans on two proteins, counted once whichever protein was the query. It is correct when both spans overlap a domain of the same Pfam family. "Overlap" has four readings (the overlap rule), and both spans must pass it.

The figure below draws the four rules on one protein. Drag the sliders or pick an example to see which rules a span passes.

Base rate: the share of matches that are correct. A score that knows nothing gets this precision everywhere in the list.

Average precision (AP)

Sort the matches from best score to worst. Each time the list reaches a correct match, take the share of matches so far that are correct (the precision). AP is the average of those shares. 1 means every correct match comes before every wrong one; a score that knows nothing gets the base rate.

ROC AUC

The chance that a randomly picked correct match scores higher than a randomly picked wrong match (ties count half). 0.5 is a coin flip, 1 is perfect. AUC does not depend on how many matches are correct, so it can look good when almost none are. —

Compared with region length

Longer matched regions are more often correct, so length alone already sorts matches well. Each metric is judged on the matches it can score, and region length is ranked on those same matches. "Minus length" is the metric's AP or AUC minus length's. Blue cells: the metric puts correct matches higher than length does. Red: lower.

Bits of seed information

The k-mer sizes were chosen so every alphabet gets the same information per k-mer: bits = k × the Shannon entropy of the alphabet's letter frequencies in the human proteome (bits per letter). A 2-letter alphabet at k = 16 and the 20 amino acids at k = 5 both carry about 16 to 21 bits.
