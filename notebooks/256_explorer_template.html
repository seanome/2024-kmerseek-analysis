BHF against 300 shuffled copies of itself

Notebooks 256 and 258. BHF (Botryllus histocompatibility factor, aa) and 300 shuffled copies of BHF were each searched with kmerseek against the canonical human proteins (GENCODE v49). A shuffled copy has BHF's length, its amino-acid counts and its count of every adjacent residue pair (a dipeptide shuffle), but any real match to a human protein is gone. Whatever a copy finds, BHF could find by chance.

An alphabet-ksize pair is one reduced amino-acid alphabet at one k-mer size, the search settings of notebook 241. Each pair ranks the human proteins under 11 ranking metrics. Pick a metric below.

How often the 2024 talk's human proteins reach the top 10

The 2024 Evolgenome talk named as BHF hits. That list was picked from BHF's own best hits in a k-mer search. Any query's own best hits come back when it is searched again and not when it is shuffled, so BHF beating its copies here is expected. It shows these hits depend on the order of BHF's residues, not that they are homologs.

For each metric: the share of alphabet-ksize pairs where at least one of these proteins ranks in the query's top 10. Tied proteins take their best rank, for BHF and copies alike. The number on the right is the share of copies at least as high as BHF, (1 + copies) / 301. The selected metric is in bold.

Do BHF's matches land on zinc fingers, membrane regions or extracellular parts?

Notebook 258. For every query, alphabet-ksize pair and ranking metric, the search kept the best region of each of the query's 10 best human proteins. A match lands on a class when at least half of its human region lies inside that class. The classes come from reviewed UniProt human entries, matched to the GENCODE proteins by identical sequence ( proteins):

  • zinc finger: UniProt zinc finger regions;
  • membrane: transmembrane and intramembrane segments, regions annotated as interacting with membranes, and lipid anchors (myristoylation, palmitoylation, prenylation sites) widened by 5 residues on each side;
  • extracellular: extracellular topological domains, and the mature chain of secreted proteins with no transmembrane segment;
  • disordered: UniProt's MobiDB-lite disordered regions. Zinc finger, membrane and extracellular are counted outside these.

Each dot is one query's share of its matches on the class, among matches in a human protein with a reviewed UniProt entry (). The number on the right is the share of copies at least as high as BHF, (1 + copies) / 301. The top block pools every metric; the lower block follows the metric picked above.
