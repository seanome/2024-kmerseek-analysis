Do the alphabets agree on BCL2?

You expected this: if Ced9's BH1 stretch is like BCL2's, several alphabets should each find a region there, and that agreement should push BCL2 up the list. Below are the real regions from notebook 241's searches of Ced9 and P66 against the 19_720 canonical human proteins, all 19 alphabets.

Short answer: the alphabets do agree on BCL2's BH1, but only four of them, and thousands of unrelated proteins get more alphabets to agree. Agreement between alphabets mostly measures how many residues are identical, and the alphabets are not independent witnesses. So agreement cannot single out a remote homolog like BCL2.

Why agreement does not help

  
    
    
  1. An identical stretch is a match in every alphabet. Grouping residues into classes never splits two identical residues, so an identical stretch is identical in all 19 alphabets, and every alphabet whose k-mers fit inside it reports it. That is why TKT gets 18 alphabets from IGAGVT and CD55 gets all 19 from KTTTPN. The alphabets are not voting independently; they are all seeing the same identical letters. Figure 3 shows this over every spot.
    2. 
    
  2. The alphabets mostly split residues the same way. Almost every alphabet keeps hydrophobic residues apart from charged ones. So a chance stretch that looks alike in one alphabet looks alike in most of them. Notebook 242 counted this: 1_200 proteins beat BCL2 in at least half of the 13 alphabets that find BCL2, 7.9 times as many as independent alphabets would give.
    3. 
    
  3. BCL2's BH1 is visible only to the coarsest alphabets, and three of its four are one alphabet. Ced9 and BCL2 share 5 of 26 residues here, with no identical run longer than 3. Only two-, three- and five-letter alphabets see it. hp_lehninger2, hp_lehninger_c_nonpolar2 and hp_lehninger_hpc3 differ only in where they put cysteine (C). Counting the seven H/P alphabets as one, BCL2 has 2, and at least 9_744 human proteins have more.
    4. 
    
  4. No alphabet calls any of these spots below E = 10. The best BH1 call is hp_lehninger2 at k = 19, E = 11_173: about 11_173 regions this good are expected by chance in one search of the human proteome. Restricted to calls with E < 10, no human protein gets more than 2 alphabets to agree, for either query. Agreement among calls that are each at chance level is still chance.

What would make agreement mean something

  
Your idea is sound when the witnesses are independent and each one has some evidence. Here neither holds. These changes would test it fairly; each needs a run, so none of them is done here.

  
    
    
  • Count only calls that pass an E-value cut in their own alphabet, then count alphabets. On these two queries that leaves at most 2 alphabets per protein, so it needs a query where single alphabets already reach E < 10.
    • 
    
  • Count alphabet families, not alphabets: the seven H/P alphabets as one, and a stretch protein20 also calls as identity rather than as extra support.
    • 
    
  • Compare against shuffled queries. Notebook 263 did this for four copies of one alphabet: real regions were called by all four 85% to 97% of the time, regions on shuffled queries 37% to 56%. The same test across the 19 alphabets would say how much agreement chance alone gives.
