# kmerseek project: where things stand on 2026-10-13

This page summarizes the work in two public repositories so that a reader without access to
the local chats can pick it up:

- [seanome/kmerseek](https://github.com/seanome/kmerseek): the tool, written in Rust.
- [seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis): the
  benchmarks, notebooks and Nextflow pipelines for the paper.

Every number below is copied from a PR description or a project note. The PR named next to
it is where the number and the code that made it live. Many analysis PRs are open drafts, so
their numbers are on the PR branch and not yet on `main`.

## What kmerseek is

kmerseek finds protein regions that share k-mers (exact runs of k letters) after the protein
is rewritten in a reduced alphabet. A reduced alphabet groups the 20 amino acids into a few
classes. The simplest has two classes, hydrophobic (H) and polar (P). There are 19 alphabets
in the tool, from 2 to 20 classes. A shared k-mer is a seed. Seeds on one diagonal (the same
offset between the two proteins) are joined into a region. Since version 0.4 a region can be
extended past its seeds, without gaps, and given a Karlin-Altschul E-value (the BLAST-style
count of hits this good expected by chance in the database).

A "setting" is one alphabet at one k-mer size, plus the search options.

## The paper story

Working story, set on 2026-09-11: kmerseek finds functional domains with no structure
prediction and no alignment. The intended claims: its accuracy does not change with disorder
or pLDDT, it is competitive on recent divergence (up to about 100 million years), it places
boundaries better for domains under 30 residues, and it finds modules in an invertebrate
protein (BHF) where BLAST and HMMER find nothing. The earlier framing, detecting homologs in the "twilight zone" of low sequence identity, was
dropped because every kmerseek setting scores near zero below 60% identity on the Pfam truth
set. The unit of evaluation is a domain interval on a protein, not a protein pair. The
target journal is Nature Biotechnology. Gaps a reviewer would raise:
the truth set is partly circular, the structural comparison tool Folddisco is outside the
conditions it was built for, and there is no null model with matched H/P composition.

Three running examples:

- **CED-9 (*C. elegans*) and BCL2 (human)**: the known homolog pair, used as the gold
  standard. They share the BH1 region.
- **P66 (*Borreliella burgdorferi*) and CD47 (human)**: a published claim that P66 mimics
  CD47. This is the stretch case.
- **BHF (Botryllus histocompatibility factor)**: the application. BLAST and HMMER find
  nothing for it. It was planned as Figure 1's example. Figure 1 now uses H/P runs instead (PR 95).

## Main results so far

### H/P keeps its pattern in related proteins, but carries little evidence per position

- At 20-30% identity (37,085 Pfam seed pairs), H/P classes agree at 73.9 of 100 aligned
  positions against 51.4 by chance. The 20 amino acids are identical at 25.2 against 6.7 by
  chance. H/P carries 0.162 bits per aligned position and the 20 amino acids 0.251
  ([PR 99](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/99), notebook 273).
- Against BLOSUM62, the substitution matrix aligners use, H/P gives about half the
  evidence per aligned position. No kmerseek alphabet beats BLOSUM62 (0 of 108 scorings).
  Among exact-match scorings, which is what a k-mer index can use, `hp_thomas_dill2` is the
  best of the 19 alphabets for SCOPe pairs that share only a fold (1.78 times protein20's
  bits). This was found after looking, so it still needs a prespecified test
  ([PR 119](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/119), notebook 278).
- Figure 1 draft: related proteins keep their H/P pattern in runs of about 12 residues
  (mean longest same-class run 12.0, median 11, at 20-30% identity), so the seed length
  trades homologs reached against chance hits. Only 13.4% of those pairs share a run of 17
  or more ([PR 95](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/95), notebook 275).

### The three examples

- **CED-9 / BCL2**: BCL2 is among CED-9's hits for 13 of 19 alphabets but never ranks
  better than 213 of 18,064 human proteins hit. Its best E-value is 1,955
  ([PR 46](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/46), notebook 241).
  Only 4 of the 19 alphabets agree on BCL2's best spot (BH1), and 4,848 human proteins get
  more alphabets agreeing. Combining alphabets does not help
  ([PR 125](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/125), notebook 284;
  [PR 48](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/48), notebook 242).
- **P66 / CD47**: CD47's best rank is 166 of 18,775, best E-value 707 (PR 46). Structure
  does not support the mimicry claim: P66's fold scores below all 20 unrelated immunoglobulin
  domains against CD47 (TM-score 0.342), Foldseek finds no CD47 entry, and the claimed loop
  is the worst fit of 11 P66 loops to CD47's binding face
  ([PR 118](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/118), notebook 276).
  The AlphaFold model of P66 has mean pLDDT 29 and there is no experimental structure.
- **BHF**: by E-value, BHF's hits do no better than dipeptide-shuffled copies of BHF (3 of
  61 settings beat 95% of copies, 3.1 expected by chance). Other scores beat the copies more
  often, mostly through one lysine-rich low-complexity stretch, BHF 63-105
  ([PR 83](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/83), notebook 256). The
  control that keeps such stretches whole is set up but not yet run
  ([PR 124](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/124), notebook 283).

So none of the three examples is yet a clean positive result for kmerseek.

### Benchmarks

- **Dark proteins** (no phmmer, jackhmmer or MMseqs2 hit in Swiss-Prot with the query's own
  clade removed): the best setting reaches 12-26% of them at E < 1, against 38-73% of
  proteins that do have a hit. The rule written before the numbers was met in all 5 species.
  But the reached dark proteins are enriched for compositional bias and transmembrane
  helices (in human, 58.7% vs 36.2% have a biased stretch), and the low-complexity mask was
  off. Next step: drop those regions and count again
  ([PR 120](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/120), notebook 279; run
  code in [PR 57](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/57)). The kmerseek
  0.4 dark-set run finished on 2026-10-12 with 8,170 tasks and none failed.
  PR 120 is the first notebook to read it.
- **Pfam domains, kmerseek vs aligners** (held-out half of 21,915 human Pfam instance x
  species rows): no held-out domain is placed only by kmerseek; there are 183 cases where
  kmerseek's boundary is tighter
  ([PR 86](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/86), merged).
- **ELM short linear motifs**: kmerseek places no motif that all the other tools miss, in
  8 species ([PR 51](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/51) and
  related).
- **Recall falls with domain length**: for the best kmerseek setting, 0.365 of domains under 50 residues are placed against 0.060
  of domains of 200 residues or more (notebook 227, merged in PR 37). Across seven kmerseek settings the fall is 3.6 to 6.1 times, while phmmer and Foldseek rise
  with length. kmerseek recall does not change with disorder and sits about 4 times below the
  baselines in every disorder band.
- **SCOPe40 with kmerseek 0.4**: 19 alphabets, every k from k_min to k_max, six search
  settings, 1,358 searches, all finished 2026-10-12
  ([PR 107](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/107), notebook 277). The best k sits at the bottom of the tested range, so lower k is needed. Measured against all
  true pairs in the database, the best kmerseek family AUC is 0.122 (hsdm17, k = 5) against
  0.403 for MMseqs2, about 30%. An earlier version of notebook 277 divided by the pairs
  kmerseek reported instead, which made kmerseek look like half to three quarters of MMseqs2.
- **Time-split Pfam test**: would kmerseek have placed domains that Pfam added between
  release 33.1 (2020) and 38.2 (2026)? Stop 1 (query set) is done; the 200-query pilot
  started on Sherlock on 2026-10-13
  ([PR 121](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/121), notebook 280).
- **Shuffled-query check**: on one dipeptide shuffle of each of the 998 queries, the zebrafish
  searches make 335 calls at E < 10, against 891 for the real queries (38%). At E < 1 it is 22
  against 673 (3%). Precision against the truth set does not separate the two
  ([PR 103](https://github.com/seanome/2024-kmerseek-analysis/pull/103), notebook 263).

## kmerseek software status

Merged since September: streaming index build, chunked search cache, `--scaled` restored,
region tf-idf and mean IDF columns, B/J/Z ambiguity codes expanded to every reading,
ungapped extension with an X-drop walk, Karlin-Altschul fit stored in the index, chaining of
regions along a diagonal, the expected shared-k-mer count scaled by target size (PR 136),
and docs pages on GitHub Pages.

Known problems: in 0.4.0, proteins with identical k-mer sets are merged as aliases and a hit
can carry the wrong target coordinates (fixed by PR 141; every 0.4.0 index must then be rebuilt). The run E-value is conservative
because run lengths are whole numbers; the mid-p version (PR 130) fixes most of it. In the searches of notebook 241, 81 of 152 settings had no
Karlin-Altschul fit, and many regions had no E-value. Part of that came from a per-region
lambda build that was later dropped (kmerseek PR 101, closed).

## Working rules a new helper should know

- The H/P alphabets matter most; protein20 and dayhoff are baselines.
- `scaled=1` (no k-mer subsampling) unless a test is about scaling.
- kmerseek region coordinates are 0-based and end-exclusive; truth sets are 1-based.
- Report query counts both for all queries and for the queries FoldSeek also covers.
- Decision rules are written before the numbers are computed. A claim states the number it
  rests on.
- Compute runs on Stanford's Sherlock cluster through Nextflow; the laptop keeps only the
  tables notebooks read.
