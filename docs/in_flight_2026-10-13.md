## The project in five lines

- kmerseek finds protein regions that share exact k-mers after the protein is rewritten in a
  reduced alphabet (19 alphabets, from 2 letters, hydrophobic/polar "H/P", to 20). A
  "setting" is one alphabet at one k-mer size.
- Paper story (Nature Biotechnology target): a structure-free, alignment-free domain finder.
- Three running examples: CED-9/BCL2 (known homologs), P66/CD47 (claimed mimicry), BHF (a
  tunicate protein with no known relatives). None is a clean positive result yet.
- Figure 1 is PR 95 (nb 275). Supplementary Figure 1 is PR 106.
- Olga reviews every PR before merge. 78 PRs are open: 67 analysis, 11 kmerseek.

## Questions worth asking another reader

- With all three examples at chance or not supported, what result can carry the paper's claim
  that kmerseek finds domains without structure?
- Is "kmerseek places tighter boundaries on short features" (183 Pfam cases, nb 244) strong
  enough to lead with, given that no domain is placed only by kmerseek?
- For the dark set, is 12-26% reach at E < 1 meaningful once the low-complexity and
  transmembrane regions are removed?
